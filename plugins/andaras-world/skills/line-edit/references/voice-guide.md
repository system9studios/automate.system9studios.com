## MUD text mechanics
- **Room short name:** 2–5 words, title case, no articles unless part of a name.
- **Room long description:** 3–6 sentences. The first sentence carries the room's identity; a player skimming reads only that. Mention exits and interactables in the prose so they're discoverable, but don't list them (the exit line does that).
- **Second person, present tense.** Describe what's there, not what the player does or feels. "The gate stands open" not "You walk through the open gate, feeling uneasy."
- **Static vs. dynamic.** Long descriptions hold what's always true. Time, weather, crowds, and NPC positions come from dynamic lines, not the base text.
- **Item text:** short description ≤ 8 words; examine text 1–3 sentences, leading with what it's for, then one detail that reveals the world.
- **Dialogue:** lines ≤ 2 sentences each. Each NPC has a verbal fingerprint (register, rhythm, a pet phrase). Exposition only as an answer to something the player asked.
- **Barks and rumors:** one line, overheard register, deniable.

## The world's voice
The world's own voice comes from canon: the world foundation page's tone guardrails and the cosmology page's terminology register. Read them before a review and cite them in canon findings. Craft defaults that hold under any guardrail:
- Specific nouns over adjectives: "a hand-riveted water tank" over "an old, rusty tank".
- Register tells you who's talking: check each speaker's terms against the terminology table.
- Mundane first. The extraordinary is rarer and lands harder for it.
- Show the world through its objects and habits; never through a history lecture.

## Common findings
- Opening with weather or light instead of the room's identity
- "You feel…", "you notice…", "you can see…"
- Stacked adjectives, "ancient", "eerie", "mysterious"
- Stating a Secret the player hasn't earned
- Every NPC sounding like the narrator
